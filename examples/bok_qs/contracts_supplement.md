# Supplementary notes — UK construction contract families

## JCT (Joint Contracts Tribunal)

The JCT family of contracts is the most common standard form used in UK
private-sector building work. Standard variants include:

- **Standard Building Contract (SBC)** for traditional procurement with bills.
- **Design and Build (DB)** where the contractor designs and builds.
- **Intermediate (IC)** for medium-sized projects of straightforward nature.
- **Minor Works (MW)** for low-value, low-complexity work.

JCT valuations follow the rules in **clause 5** (Variations), with daywork
applying when no fair adjustment of rates is possible. The Architect or
Contract Administrator issues instructions; the Quantity Surveyor values.

## NEC (New Engineering Contract)

The NEC family is the dominant standard in UK public-sector infrastructure
work. The core contract is the **NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract
(ECC)**. NEC uses a different vocabulary and behavioural philosophy:

- The **Project Manager (PM)** is the contract administrator role.
- Changes are handled via the **Compensation Event (CE)** procedure
  (clauses 60–66). A CE captures both time and cost effects.
- The **Activity Schedule** or **Bill of Quantities** is selected by the
  main Option (A or B for priced contracts, C/D for target contracts).
- Early-warning notices are mandatory and time-bound under clause 15.

## When to cite which standard

When advising on measurement, cite **NRM2** for the rules governing what is
measured and how units are derived. When advising on payment mechanics or
the validity of an instruction, cite the **contract clause** of the specific
JCT or NEC form in use. Conflating the two is a common error and undermines
the audit trail required by RICS Rule 2 (competence) and Rule 5 (clear and
adequate communication).

## Worked example: small variation under JCT SBC

Background: a wall finish is changed from emulsion paint to a wallpaper
specification mid-construction. The Contract Administrator issues a written
Architect's Instruction. The QS:

1. Confirms the AI references the changed work clearly and is in the form
   required by the contract.
2. Measures the affected area per NRM2 Section 28 (Painting, decorating and
   wall coverings).
3. Builds up a rate from first principles using current supplier quotations
   for wallpaper material and a labour all-in rate; cites both sources.
4. Adjusts preliminaries only if the change has a measurable programme effect.
5. Submits the valuation with the AI reference, the NRM2 measurement note,
   the rate build-up, and a one-line statement that the change has no
   programme effect (or the programme effect quantified).

## Worked example: Compensation Event under NEC4 ECC Option A

Background: an unforeseen ground condition is encountered. The Contractor
issues an early warning under clause 15, then a CE notification under
clause 61.3. The QS, acting for the Project Manager:

1. Confirms the CE category (here: clause 60.1(12), physical conditions).
2. Reviews the quotation submitted by the Contractor against the **Defined
   Cost + Fee** rules in Section 5, applying the Schedule of Cost Components.
3. Verifies the Contractor's programme submission shows the resulting
   planned completion change.
4. Recommends to the PM whether to accept the quotation, propose an
   alternative, or make the PM's own assessment under clause 64.
